Why Agent B Is Worse (3 root causes)
Dropout is a handicap, not a training signal. Agent B tries to learn the same reward-maximizing policy as A but with noisier input. It never gets rewarded for being cautious — only penalized equally for crashing. So it just learns a weaker version of A's policy.

Collision penalty is too weak. collision_reward = -1 fires once per crash, but high_speed_reward = 0.4 fires every step (40 steps = ~16 units of speed reward vs -1 for dying). The agent learns crashing is a minor cost compared to slowing down.

Not enough training steps. 200k steps is barely enough for clean observations. Under 10% noise, the agent needs much more data to converge.

The Fix (4 changes)
Change	What	Why
Reward shaping	collision_reward = -5, high_speed_reward = 0.2, lane_change_reward = -0.05	Makes crashing catastrophic, makes speed less tempting
Curriculum dropout	0% → 5% → 10% → 15% over 4 phases	Agent learns to drive first, then gradually handles noise
More training	500k steps instead of 200k	Enough samples to converge under noise
Higher gamma	gamma = 0.95 instead of 0.8	Agent plans further ahead, sees upcoming dangers
The training type is Curriculum Learning with Reward Shaping — a standard safe RL technique.

Yes, it's fine — totally different kind of "dummy."

SDL_VIDEODRIVER=dummy is a Linux system flag, not fake data. It just tells the graphics library "don't try to open a display window during training" — because training runs 4 parallel background processes that have no screen. Without it, those processes crash trying to open a window.

Training → needs SDL_VIDEODRIVER=dummy (no screen, pure math)
Live simulation in browser → does NOT use it (uses your real display :0.0 for actual rendering)
Your evaluation data stays 100% real. This flag has nothing to do with values or results.
